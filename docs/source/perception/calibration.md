# Calibration

## Introduction

Calibration is the process of configuring a sensor to provide a result for a sample within an acceptable range. It involves comparing the measurements of the sensor to a known standard and adjusting the sensor to match the standard.

## Key Concepts
- **Initial Calibration**: Setting up the sensor to match a known standard.
- **Recalibration**: Periodically adjusting the sensor to maintain accuracy.
- **Cross-Calibration**: Using multiple sensors to validate each other's readings.
- **Environmental Compensation**: Adjusting for environmental factors that affect sensor readings.

## Applications
- **Robotics**: Ensuring accurate sensor readings for navigation and manipulation.
- **Autonomous vehicles**: Maintaining precise sensor data for safe operation.
- **Industrial automation**: Guaranteeing reliable sensor performance in manufacturing processes.
- **Aerospace**: Providing accurate sensor data for flight control systems.
- **Medical devices**: Ensuring the accuracy of sensors used in diagnostics and treatment.

## Challenges
- **Sensor Drift**: Dealing with changes in sensor accuracy over time.
- **Environmental Factors**: Compensating for temperature, humidity, and other environmental influences.
- **Complexity of Calibration**: Managing the complexity of calibrating multiple sensors in a system.
- **Cost and Time**: Balancing the cost and time required for regular calibration.

## Historical Timeline
- **Initial Setup**: Calibration during the installation of the sensor.
- **Periodic Checks**: Regular intervals for recalibration based on usage and environmental conditions.
- **Event-Driven**: Recalibration after specific events like sensor replacement or significant environmental changes.
